# Does Equivariance Beat Exact Augmentation? Transferring Equivariant Diffusion Policy to Planar Pushing with Controllable Symmetry

**Members:** [Name A], [Name B], [Name C] **Base paper:** Wang et al., *Equivariant Diffusion Policy* (EquiDiff), CoRL 2024

**Research question & motivation.** EquiDiff builds SO(2) symmetry into the denoiser of Diffusion Policy (DP). It proves that the noise-prediction function is equivariant whenever the expert policy is, and reports +21.9 pp over DP on 12 MimicGen tasks with 100 demos. We see three gaps. (i) All tasks are 6-DoF tabletop manipulation seen from an *oblique* camera, where rotating the image is not the same as rotating the scene (the authors' own stated limitation). (ii) So App. I's finding "equivariant net > rotation augmentation" (53.7 vs 46.2) compares against an augmentation that produces physically wrong samples; architecture and augmentation quality are confounded. (iii) Symmetry strength is grouped only qualitatively, and *incorrect* equivariance is discussed but never tested. The follow-up paper (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2025) simplifies how symmetry is added (Frame Averaging, invariant representations), but it still evaluates only on MimicGen/Robomimic and does not separate these factors. Following the instructor's advice, we move EquiDiff to a **different task family: Push-T** (planar, non-prehensile, contact-rich, SE(2) positional actions). We verified in the DP code that Push-T has zero gravity, a square arena with walls symmetric to within 0.5 px, a top-down camera, and a goal at the arena centre (256, 256, π/4). So if the goal is rotated along with the scene, a rotation by k·90° is an *exact* symmetry of both the dynamics and the image (`rot90` is lossless). That makes Push-T a testbed where symmetry is exact, can be broken on demand, and augmentation can be exact. **RQ1 (transfer)**: Does EquiDiff's data-efficiency gain carry over to planar pushing? **RQ2 (mechanism)**: Once exact C4 augmentation is available, does the equivariant architecture still beat DP + exact augmentation? **RQ3 (failure & fix)**: When the symmetry is broken (a fixed goal the policy cannot observe → incorrect equivariance), how much does EquiDiff lose, and can our fixes recover it?

**Proposed approach.** (1) *Push-T-Rot environment*: the goal angle is π/4 + kπ/2 with k ~ U{0..3}, and the initial-state sampler is re-centred on the arena centre (the original is off by ≈6 px). The 206 human demos are rotated consistently (image, agent, T-pose, actions): the demo count is unchanged, but each orientation gets only ¼ of the data. (2) *Port EquiDiff*: a C4 (and C8) equivariant ResNet encoder on 96×96 images; agent position and each 2-D action step typed as ρ1 around the centre; the paper's weight-tied temporal U-Net applied per group element. (3) *Fixes for broken symmetry*: (a) **goal-as-input**, where the goal heading is fed as an extra ρ1 feature so the problem becomes correctly equivariant again; (b) **learnable symmetry breaking**, where a world-frame reference vector is added as a ρ1 input through a zero-initialised gate (in the spirit of approximately equivariant networks, Wang et al. ICML 2022), so training starts exactly equivariant and breaks symmetry only as the data demands. *Optional E4*: train the 6-DoF EquiDiff and DP on MimicGen D0 and test on D1/D2, a quantitative version of the paper's qualitative App. M.

**Data, simulator, compute.** Push-T (pymunk) and its demos from the DP codebase; MimicGen datasets for E4 only. **One RTX 5090 (32 GB)**; EquiDiff's default batch of 128 needs ≈22 GB. All arms share a fixed iteration budget. The follow-up paper reports that EquiDiff (Im) costs ≈4× DP's GPU-hours, so we budget ≈1 GPU-h per DP run and ≈4 per EquiDiff run. Planned runs: RQ1/2 with 4 data sizes (25/50/100/206) × {DP, DP+exact-aug, EquiDiff-C4} × 3 seeds, plus C8 at 2 sizes; RQ3 with fixed/rotated goal × {DP, Equi, Equi+goal, Equi+gate} × 3 seeds on state inputs, plus 18 image runs. Total ≈ 200 GPU-h ≈ 9 GPU-days. **Go/no-go at week 2**: if measured costs project above 300 GPU-h, we keep C4 only, use 3 data sizes, and drop E4.

**Evaluation.** Push-T coverage (IoU with the goal) and success (coverage ≥ 0.95) on 100 fixed test seeds (25 per goal orientation). Primary metric: final-checkpoint score (mean of the last 3 evaluations). We also report the paper's max-over-training metric for comparability and discuss its optimistic bias. We use 3 training seeds, paired-by-seed bootstrap 95% CIs, data-efficiency curves, per-orientation breakdowns, and a numerical equivariance-error unit test ‖f(gx) − g f(x)‖. No paper number is used as a baseline; every arm is retrained under one protocol. *Pre-registered predictions*: H1: EquiDiff > DP at ≤50 demos. H2: the gap to DP+exact-aug is much smaller than in App. I, possibly not significant. H3: with fixed-goal state input, plain Equi < DP, and goal-input recovers ≥ DP.

**Feasibility & risks.** (i) Blackwell needs torch ≥ 2.7/CUDA 12.8, the official environment pins older versions, and escnn has fragile dependencies. *Fallback*: exact C4 equivariance via Frame Averaging (Puny et al., ICLR 2022; 4 forward passes, no escnn), which the follow-up paper also uses. (ii) The physics may not be exactly symmetric; we check this by open-loop replay of rotated demos and confirming coverage matches. (iii) DP Push-T officially trains for ~3000 epochs; we use a fixed budget with a convergence check on the DP arm. (iv) H2 may come out null, which is still informative. (v) Novelty rests on our own literature search and will be re-checked before the final paper.
